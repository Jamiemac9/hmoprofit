From 15 December 2026, landlords in England must register themselves and each property they let on the government's new Register your rental property service, starting in the West Midlands. The fee is £65 per property per year, and a licensed HMO counts as one property, so a shared house pays one fee, not one per room.

## What has been announced

GOV.UK published its guidance for landlords on 29 October 2026 and updated it on 4 November 2026. Registration opens region by region, and each region gets a three-month window. The deadline depends on where the property is, not where you live.

| Region | Registration opens | Deadline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| West Midlands | 15 December 2026 | 14 March 2027 |
| East of England | 15 January 2027 | 14 April 2027 |
| East Midlands | 15 February 2027 | 14 May 2027 |
| South East | 15 March 2027 | 14 June 2027 |
| Yorkshire and Humber | 15 April 2027 | 14 July 2027 |
| North West | 15 May 2027 | 14 August 2027 |
| North East | 15 June 2027 | 14 September 2027 |
| London | 15 July 2027 | 14 October 2027 |
| South West | 15 August 2027 | 14 November 2027 |

## What it costs, and how an HMO is counted

The fee is £65 per property per year, with no VAT. You pay it when you register each property and then every year after that. Registering yourself as a landlord is free, and so is updating a record. During the rollout the fee is pro-rated, so registering early does not cost more.

For HMO landlords the key line is this: if the property has an HMO licence, you register the whole property once. You do not register each room.

A worked example: for a five-bedroom licensed HMO, £65 a year is £13 per room per year, or about £5.42 a month for the whole house. That is small next to licence fees and safety certificates, but it is a new fixed cost that comes round every year, so it belongs in your running costs.

## What you need ready

- A GOV.UK One Login, which you need to use the service
- Property details: address, number of bedrooms and residents, and the rent you receive
- Your gas safety record (if the property has gas) and your electrical certificate (EICR or EIC), with their dates
- Your EPC, or if the rating is below the minimum, whether an exemption applies
- Any licences, including your HMO licence

You can add some of the information within 28 days of submitting. If you don't, the property's registration may be deactivated. If an agent manages the house, they can upload some of the details, but you still have to start the registration, and you are responsible for the record being complete within 28 days.

## What happens if you don't register

The guidance sets fines of up to £7,000 for failing to register yourself or a property, and up to £40,000 for knowingly or recklessly giving false or misleading information, or for continuing or repeated breaches. Councils can take enforcement action once a region's deadline has passed. From 15 December 2026, tenants can also apply for Rent Repayment Orders in areas where registration has opened.

## If your HMO is in the West Midlands

1. Check the dates on your gas safety record, electrical certificate and EPC now. Out-of-date paperwork is the most likely thing to hold you up.
2. Find your HMO licence details.
3. Set up your GOV.UK One Login.
4. Register between 15 December 2026 and 14 March 2027.
5. Add £65 a year per property to your running costs.
